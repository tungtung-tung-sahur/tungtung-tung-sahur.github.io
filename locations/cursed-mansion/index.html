Cursed Mansion

The final zone. Narrow corridors, locked doors, and the wooden character moving at full speed. The Golden Statue is buried somewhere inside. Only 12% of players reach this zone in a single run — and most of them never make it out.

🏰 What the Cursed Mansion Actually Is

The zone looks like an old manor. In practice, it is a corridor maze with one exit and one treasure. Every door you open makes noise. Every corridor you enter can become a dead end. The patrol loop is shorter here than anywhere else — roughly 15 seconds — and the wooden character no longer slows down for anything.

The first three zones gave you space to recover from mistakes. The Dark Forest gave you time to learn. The Graveyard gave you fog but open ground. The Abandoned Village gave you four exit routes. The Cursed Mansion gives you none of that. It is the only zone where a wrong turn is not recoverable. If you enter a corridor that ends in a locked door and the patrol is behind you, you lose a life. There is no crouching out of it. There is no waiting it out. You either find a side room before he reaches you, or you restart at the truck.

The zone has two purposes. The first is to hide the Golden Statue. The second is to test whether you spent your detector pulses wisely. If you saved at least 10 pulses for this zone, you can find the statue in under two minutes. If you burned them all in the Forest, you will be digging blind in corridors while the patrol circles you — and the game knows it. The statue emits a faint metallic hum that becomes louder as you approach it. If you listen carefully, you can find it without the detector. But the hum is quiet, and the patrol's footsteps are loud.

The Cursed Mansion is where the run ends — one way or the other. There is no fifth zone. There is no next map. When you find the statue and dig it up, you must carry it back to the truck. The walk back is longer than you think.

🚪 The Corridors and Doors: How They Work

Every door in the Mansion has three states: closed, open, and locked. Closed doors can be pushed. Open doors stay open. Locked doors require finding a key — or forcing them, which makes noise. Every interaction produces a sound event that the patrol can hear.

🚪

Opening a Door

Opening a door takes about 1.5 seconds and produces a short creak. The patrol reacts to this sound if he is within 12 meters. Doors stay open once opened — you cannot close them behind you. Every door you open is a permanent change to the map.

🔒

Locked Doors

Locked doors require a key. There are three keys in the Mansion, each in a different wing. Forcing a locked door takes 4 seconds and produces a loud bang that draws the patrol from up to 25 meters away. Never force a door unless you know the patrol is on the opposite side of the map.

🌬️

Cross-Draft Corridors

Some corridors connect two wings and have windows on both ends. These corridors carry sound. If you open a door at one end, the patrol hears it at the other end — even if he is 20 meters away. Avoid cross-draft corridors during the patrol's approach.

🕯️

Lit vs Unlit

Lit corridors have oil lamps and better visibility. Unlit corridors are darker but quieter — footsteps do not echo as much. In unlit corridors, the patrol's approach is harder to hear. In lit corridors, you can see him coming from further away.

🗺️ The Four Wings

The Mansion has four wings — North, South, East, and West. Each has a different layout, a different key, and a different clue. The torn map fragment from the Abandoned Village tells you which wing holds the Golden Statue.

1

North Wing

Wide corridors with tall windows. The easiest wing to navigate but the most exposed. The key here is in a study at the far end. Contains one of the three Mansion keys and a buried relic.

2

South Wing

Narrow corridors with many side rooms. The most confusing wing — easy to get lost in. The key is in a bedroom on the second floor. Contains the most hiding spots, but also the most dead ends.

3

East Wing

A single long hallway with doors on both sides. The key is behind the farthest door. Very exposed — if the patrol enters this wing while you are inside, you have nowhere to hide except the rooms. Each room has one exit.

4

West Wing

The largest wing, with a grand staircase and a ballroom. The key is under the staircase. This is the most visually impressive wing but also the loudest — the floorboards creak under every step.

🏆 The Golden Statue: Final Dig

The Golden Statue is buried in the wing indicated by your torn map fragment. If you did not retrieve the fragment from the Abandoned Village, you will need to sweep all four wings with the metal detector — using 15 to 20 pulses that you cannot get back.

The statue emits a slow, low-frequency hum. The hum grows louder as you approach the correct spot. Within 3 meters of the statue, the hum is audible over footsteps. Within 1 meter, it is the loudest sound in the room. If you are listening carefully, you can find the statue without the metal detector — but this is risky in a zone where the patrol is always close.

Digging up the statue takes 6 seconds. During those 6 seconds, you cannot move. The dig produces a continuous scraping sound that draws the patrol from up to 15 meters away. You must start the dig only when the patrol is on the opposite side of the Mansion — not just out of sight, but two wings away.

Once the statue is in your hands, the run is not over. You must carry it back to the truck. Carrying the statue slows your movement by 20% and eliminates your ability to crouch. You must walk, not run, and you cannot hide behind low cover. Every door you opened on the way in is still open. Every corridor you cleared is now a threat again.

"The statue weighs nothing. But you will feel every step back to the truck."

In-game hint shown when the statue is first lifted

🧠 How to Survive the Mansion

Five tactics that reduce the risk of losing a life in this zone. None of them are optional. All of them are learnable in three or four runs.

1️⃣

Find the Keys Before the Statue

Do not dig for the statue until you have at least two of the three keys. If you dig first and then need a key, you will be carrying a heavy statue through locked doors. Get the keys first.

2️⃣

Open Doors on the Way In, Not the Way Out

Open every door you might need to pass through on your return trip while you are still empty-handed. Opening doors is fast when you are not carrying the statue. It is slow and noisy when you are.

3️⃣

Use Unlit Corridors for Movement

The patrol's footsteps echo in lit corridors. In unlit corridors, sound is dampened. Use unlit corridors to move between wings — even if the route is longer.

4️⃣

Dig Only When He's Two Wings Away

The 6-second dig is the most dangerous action in the game. Start it only when you can confirm via sound that the patrol is in a wing separated from yours by at least one door. Do not guess. Listen.

5️⃣

Save the Last Life for the Exit

If you lose a life while carrying the statue, you drop it. You must return to the same spot to pick it up. If you have only one life left when you start the return trip, consider whether the run is winnable. Often it is not.

🚫 Three Mistakes That End Runs in the Mansion

These are the three behaviors that account for the highest number of failures in the final zone. All three are avoidable with the right preparation.

🔒

Forcing Locked Doors

Forcing a door makes a loud bang that draws the patrol from 25 meters away. If you are in a wing with no side room, that noise is a death sentence. Always find the key instead.

⛏️

Digging Too Early

The 6-second dig is long. If you start it while the patrol is one wing away, he will arrive before you finish. He will not wait. He will catch you mid-dig, and you will lose the life and the progress.

🏃

Running While Carrying the Statue

The statue slows you by 20% and removes your crouch. If you try to sprint while carrying it, you make more noise and gain almost no speed. The correct pace is a fast walk — nothing more.
