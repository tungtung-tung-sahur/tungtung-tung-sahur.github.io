Coin Cache

In Tung Tung Sahur, a buried coin cache is worth 15 to 25 coins and the metal detector finds it in a few taps. Sounds efficient — until you remember that every pulse you spend on a cache is a pulse you cannot spend in the Graveyard or the Cursed Mansion, where the relics that actually end the run are buried.

Coins per Perfect Loaf

A coin cache in Tung Tung Sahur is worth roughly two perfect loaves of Ramadan bread. It takes about three detector pulses to locate and two seconds to dig. On paper, that sounds efficient. In practice, the comparison is not that simple — because the two income sources compete for different resources.

Three perfect loaves take about 90 seconds including ingredient collection and they cost zero detector pulses. A coin cache takes about 20 seconds total but costs 3 to 5 pulses from a battery that has only 40 in it. Every pulse you spend on a cache is a pulse you cannot spend in the Graveyard hunting the bone charm or in the Cursed Mansion locating the Golden Statue.

That trade-off is the entire question. Coin caches are not bad. They are just competing with the bread economy — and while you are crouched over a cache, the wooden kentongan is still walking his patrol loop.

📍 Where Caches Spawn in Tung Tung Sahur

Coin caches appear in only two of the four Tung Tung Sahur zones. Knowing which zones to expect them in saves you from scanning the wrong areas.

🌲

Dark Forest — Near Cabins

The Dark Forest contains coin caches near the cabin structures. They are the first buried relics you can find in a run. Most players encounter their first cache in the Forest within the first two minutes — right where they should be baking instead.

🪦

Graveyard — South Row

The Graveyard contains coin caches in the south row of graves. They are the least valuable relic in the zone — the bone charm is the one you actually want. If you already have the charm, caches are a reasonable secondary target. If you do not, skip them until you do.

🚫

Not in the Village or Mansion

Coin caches do not spawn in the Abandoned Village or the Cursed Mansion. Both of those zones contain only story items, keys, and the Golden Statue. Do not waste pulses scanning for caches in the final two zones — they are not there.

🔍

Found Only by Detector

Caches are buried. They do not appear on the surface and they do not glow. The only way to find one in Tung Tung Sahur is to sweep the ground with the metal detector and follow the directional arrow. The signal is a sharp, high-frequency spike — different from the slow throb of the bone charm and the steady pulse of a battery.

⚖️ Coin Cache vs Bread: The Real Math

Both sources generate coins in Tung Tung Sahur. One costs pulses, the other costs time. Here is the side-by-side breakdown.

🪙

Coin Cache

Pulse Cost
  • Worth 15–25 coins per cache
  • Costs 3–5 detector pulses to locate
  • Requires 2 seconds of exposed digging
  • Puts you near the kentongan during the sweep
  • Not available in the final two zones
  • Every pulse spent here is a pulse lost in the Mansion
🥖

Ramadan Bread

Zero Pulses
  • Worth 12 coins per perfect loaf
  • Costs zero detector pulses
  • Requires about 90 seconds for three loaves
  • Takes place at the truck — the safest spot in Tung Tung Sahur
  • Available every run, in every zone
  • Compounds with no resource cost

The bread economy wins on every metric except raw coin value per action. A coin cache is worth slightly more than one loaf. But a loaf costs nothing except time. A cache costs pulses, and pulses are the single most constrained resource in Tung Tung Sahur.

That is why experienced players bake first, bake always, and treat coin caches as a bonus — something to grab when the detector happens to point at one during a sweep you were already doing for a more important relic.

📊 Pulse Cost in Tung Tung Sahur: What You Spend vs What You Keep

The detector gives you 40 pulses per run. Here is what a cache sweep actually costs in the context of a full Tung Tung Sahur run.

Action Pulses Spent Value Gained Pulse Efficiency
Find one coin cache 3–5 pulses 15–25 coins ~4 coins per pulse
Find the bone charm 10–15 pulses Wall-piercing vision for rest of run Run-defining
Find a battery recharge 2–3 pulses +15 pulses Net +12 pulses
Find the Golden Statue (no charm) 15–20 pulses Win condition Essential
Bake three perfect loaves 0 pulses 36 coins Infinite

The table makes the priority order obvious. The bone charm is worth any number of pulses. Battery recharges pay for themselves and then some. The Golden Statue is the win condition. Coin caches are the only entry on the list that is a genuine trade-off — and they are the lowest priority.

If you have fewer than 15 pulses left when you enter the Graveyard, skip coin caches entirely. You need those pulses for the bone charm. If you have more than 25 pulses after finding the charm, caches become reasonable filler targets while you wait for the kentongan to move on.

🎯 Should You Dig This Cache?

Three scenarios. Match your current Tung Tung Sahur run state to one of these cards.

✅ Dig It

You Have Plenty of Pulses

If you have already found the bone charm and still have 20+ pulses remaining, a cache is a good use of three taps. The coins go toward a lucky charm for your next run — and you were going to sweep that ground anyway.

🚫 Skip It

You Are Saving for the Charm

If you have not yet found the bone charm in the Graveyard, do not spend a single pulse on a coin cache. The charm changes the entire rest of the run. A cache gives you 20 coins. The charm gives you the statue.

⚖️ Depends

You Are Running Low

If you have 10–15 pulses left and you are still in the Dark Forest, a cache is fine — you are not reaching the Cursed Mansion anyway. If you are in the Graveyard with the same pulse count, skip the cache and go straight for the charm.

💡 Two Habits That Save Pulses

Both of these are simple. Both make a measurable difference over ten Tung Tung Sahur runs.

🎯

Dig First, Scan Later

If the detector points at something and you are already standing on top of it, dig immediately. Do not tap the detector a second time to confirm. The arrow pointed — you are there. A second tap is a wasted pulse.

📏

Three Steps Between Taps

The detector's effective radius is about four meters. Walking three steps between taps covers ground without overlapping. Two steps wastes pulses on ground you already scanned. Four steps risks skipping the space between scan radii.
