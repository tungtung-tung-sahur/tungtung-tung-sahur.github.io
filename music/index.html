🔊 The Original Audio

Every version of the meme traces back to one sound. The original "tung tung tung sahur" audio was uploaded with the first TikTok clip and has since been saved, remixed, and reused across hundreds of thousands of videos.

The Original Voice-Over

An Indonesian text-to-speech voice. It begins with nine repetitions of "tung" — the onomatopoeia for a kentongan drum being struck — followed by "sahur." The full phrase is "Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Sahur." The voice is flat, robotic, and slightly too calm for what it is describing.

The Nine-Tung Rhythm

The nine "tungs" are not accidental. They mimic the actual rhythm of a kentongan drum being struck during Ramadan. Each "tung" is evenly spaced, roughly one beat per second. The rhythm is simple enough to hum from memory after hearing it once, which is why it spread so fast.

🎤 Character Themes

Five tracks are dedicated entirely to specific characters. Each one uses a distinct instrument and tempo to match the character's design. These themes play during special events or cameo appearances.

Tung Tung Sahur — "Wooden Heartbeat"

The signature theme of the main character. Built entirely from wooden percussion instruments — wooden blocks, wood drums, and a hollow log. No electronic sounds. The rhythm mirrors the nine-tung pattern of the original audio, but slowed to half tempo.

Tralalero Tralala — "Three Legs, Three Chords"

A three-chord electric guitar riff, played three times in a row. The joke is the same as the character's design — the number three is everywhere. The theme is short, punchy, and designed to loop without getting annoying.

Bombardiro Crocodilo — "Flight Path"

Military march drums combined with the low roar of a World War II bomber engine. The track builds from a quiet takeoff to a full-volume bombing run. It is the loudest character theme in the entire catalogue.

Ballerina Cappuccina — "Caffeine Waltz"

Classical violin waltz overlaid with the soft chime of porcelain. The track is elegant and slightly wrong — the string section is professionally recorded but the percussion is made entirely from ceramic coffee cups. The result is beautiful and unsettling at the same time.

🔀 Community Remixes

Fans have created over a hundred distinct remixes of the original audio. These are the four most popular styles that appear across TikTok, YouTube, and Spotify fan uploads.

Bass Boosted

The original nine-tung rhythm with heavy sub-bass layered underneath. The result sounds like a horror movie trailer. This style dominates TikTok edits that treat Tung Tung Sahur as a genuine threat rather than a meme.

Phonk Remix

Brazilian phonk style with distorted cowbells and aggressive bass. The nine-tung pattern is stretched and pitched down to match the phonk tempo. This is the most common remix style in dance edits.

Nightcore / Speed-Up

The original audio pitched up and sped to 1.25x or 1.5x. The result is frantic and cartoonish — a perfect match for the "Building a House" trend where the character does increasingly absurd tasks at impossible speed.

Orchestral Remix

A full string orchestra performing the nine-tung rhythm as if it were a classical composition. The contrast between the absurd source material and the serious execution is the joke. This style appears most often on YouTube, not TikTok.
