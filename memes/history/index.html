The History of Tung Tung Tung Sahur

One TikTok clip. One AI-generated image. One baseball bat. Here is the complete chronological history of the meme — from a single Indonesian Ramadan tradition to a global brainrot phenomenon, a Fortnite skin, and a federal lawsuit.

🇮🇩 The Cultural Origin

Before Tung Tung Sahur was a meme, he was a sound. In Indonesia and Malaysia, a large traditional drum called a bedug or kentongan is struck during Ramadan to wake people for sahur, the pre-dawn meal before fasting. The rhythm of the drum is repetitive and rhythmic — "tung, tung, tung."

The phrase "Tung tung tung Sahur" is a popular Indonesian saying used to alert fellow Muslims that it is time for sahur. One of the earliest known instances of the phrase online is a July 13, 2013, tweet by X user @rayaaasl that read: "Sahur sahur tung tung tung, sahur sahur tung tung tung, sahur sahurrr."

The character itself is an anthropomorphic kentongan — a hollow wooden slit drum. In the meme, he is depicted as a wooden figure with large, unblinking eyes, thin cartoonish limbs, and a baseball bat. Historically, kentongans are struck with a wooden mallet. The meme replaced the mallet with a baseball bat, giving the character a more aggressive silhouette.

🎬 The First Video

On February 28, 2025 — one day before Ramadan began in Indonesia — TikTok creator Noxa (@noxaasht) posted the first known Tung Tung Sahur video. It was 24 seconds long, AI-generated, and instantly viral.

The Original Clip

A wooden stick-man holding a baseball bat, standing at what appears to be a train or bus stop at night. An Indonesian text-to-speech voice-over plays. The clip was created using DALL-E and basic AI video tools.

The Voice-Over

The original narration translates to: "Tung tung tung tung sahur. Scary anomaly that only comes out at Sahur. It is said that if someone is called for Sahur three times and does not answer, then this creature comes to your house. It's very scary."

⏳ Complete Timeline

Every key moment that shaped the meme, from the first viral clip to the federal lawsuit. Dates are exact where verified.

February 28, 2025 — The Beginning

Noxa Posts the First Video

Indonesian TikTok creator Noxa (@noxaasht) uploads the first Tung Tung Sahur video. It is 24 seconds long, AI-generated, and posted one day before Ramadan begins. The character is a wooden figure with a baseball bat, accompanied by an Indonesian voice-over. The video goes viral, eventually surpassing 49 million views and 3.5 million likes.

March 2025 — First Remixes

Lore Expansion and Fan Art

TikTok users begin creating their own edits. On March 10, @redbluzx_tiktoq posts a video of the stickman swinging his bat and exploding. On March 22, @drw.artz posts a hand-drawn version using a mechanical pencil. The lore grows: Tung Tung Sahur is a punishment for ignoring sahur calls. Peak meme popularity is recorded as March 2025.

Late March 2025 — Ramadan Farewell

The Seasonal Goodbye

As Ramadan draws to a close, Indonesian users create memes imagining Tung Tung Sahur returning home after fulfilling his duty. Videos show him walking away, boarding imaginary flights, or dramatically vanishing. On March 22, @sebuahvideorandom2 posts a farewell tribute that receives over 1.5 million views.

May 2025 — Roblox Explosion

Steal a Brainrot Launches

The Roblox game Steal a Brainrot launches. Tung Tung Sahur is added as a Rare-tier collectible. The game becomes one of the most-played Roblox games ever, reaching over 24 million concurrent players at its peak. The character becomes a commercial asset worth millions.

May 28, 2025 — Political Cameo

Orbán Viktor Posts Tung Tung Sahur

Hungarian Prime Minister Orbán Viktor posts a dancing Tung Tung Sahur video from the Carmelite Monastery negotiating room on his TikTok account, aimed at young voters. The meme has officially entered politics.

September 13, 2025 — The Removal

Tung Tung Sahur Removed from Steal a Brainrot

YouTuber Forrest Waldron (Kreekcraft) notices during a stream that Tung Tung Sahur has vanished from Steal a Brainrot. Players who owned the character are given a Gangster Footera as a replacement. The removal happens after Mementum Labs, a French licensing agency representing Noxa, reaches out to the game's developer.

September 15, 2025 — Mementum's Statement

The Agency Responds

Mementum Labs posts a statement on TikTok claiming they did not remove the character. They state that Sammy, the game's developer, removed it after being directed to their lawyer. Mementum claims Steal a Brainrot has made "millions in revenue" using Noxa's character and that they deserve a portion of the profits.

November 2025 — The Lawsuit

Spyder Games Sues Mementum Lab

Spyder Games, the developer behind Steal a Brainrot, files a lawsuit against Mementum Lab in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. Spyder argues that Mementum cannot claim copyright rights to AI-generated characters like Tung Tung Sahur because they fail the human-authorship requirement. The case tests the limits of copyright and trademark law for AI-generated content.

December 6, 2025 — The Return

Tung Tung Sahur Returns to Steal a Brainrot

After two months of removal, Tung Tung Sahur returns to Steal a Brainrot. The character is reintroduced during an Admin Abuse session with a completely new interpretation. The return is celebrated by the community but the legal dispute continues.

January 28, 2026 — AI-Free Art Trend

The Meta-Meme Begins

A Reddit user posts a screenshot of a Discord message reading: "Found out that tung tung sahur is ai generated so I made an ai free version you can use instead." The attached image is a hand-drawn cartoon version of Tung Tung Sahur. The post goes viral with over 2,100 upvotes. The trend sparks a wave of hand-drawn Tung Tung Sahur art across X and TikTok.

April 3, 2026 — Fortnite

Tung Tung Sahur Comes to Fortnite

Epic Games releases Tung Tung Sahur and Ballerina Cappuccina as official cosmetic skins in Fortnite as part of the Brainrot Bundle. The bundle costs 2,400 V-Bucks and includes a Tung Thunker pickaxe. The release is an official partnership with Mementum Labs. The skin becomes one of the most controversial items in Fortnite history, with some players calling for a boycott.

April 2026 — Counterclaims

Mementum Files Counterclaims

Mementum Labs files a counterclaim to Spyder Games' lawsuit, significantly changing the nature of the case. Mementum asserts that Tung Tung Sahur has protectable, human-created elements and that they have valid trademark rights. The battle expands beyond copyright law into trademark law.

April 30, 2026 — Building a House

The "Building a House" Trend

TikToker @brainrot.diy posts an AI-generated video of Tung Tung Sahur building a house inside a watermelon on the beach. The video gains over 705,000 views in a month. The trend explodes throughout May 2026, with Tung Tung Sahur building homes inside lemons, oranges, other brainrot characters, and even people's heads. The most viral example — building a home inside a lemon — garners over 5.4 million views.

March 30, 2026 — Triple T Dance

The Viral Dance Trend

TikToker @.memescreens uploads a CapCut template featuring Tung Tung Sahur dancing to "Dumb wit it (get in dea)" by Geaux Teezy. The video receives over 1.8 million views and 186,800 likes in two weeks. The dance becomes one of the character's most recognizable modern formats.

⚖️ The Legal Battle Explained

The dispute over Tung Tung Sahur is testing the limits of copyright and trademark law for AI-generated content. At stake is not just one character, but the entire legal framework for AI-generated memes.

Spyder Games' argument: Mementum Labs cannot claim copyright rights to AI-generated characters like Tung Tung Sahur because they fail the human-authorship requirement. Under U.S. copyright law, works generated by AI without sufficient human authorship are not copyrightable. Since Tung Tung Sahur originated from a DALL-E image, Spyder argues no valid copyright exists.

Mementum Labs' argument: Noxa made human creative contributions — he chose the character's name, wrote the prompts, selected the output, and built the character's backstory. Mementum compares this to historical viral memes like Keyboard Cat, where the human creator successfully asserted ownership. They also point to a "thriving commercial licensing program" as proof that Tung Tung Sahur is a trademark.

The stakes: Nearly every character in Steal a Brainrot is based on an AI-generated image from an external creator. If a court rules that Mementum Labs holds enforceable rights to Tung Tung Sahur, it could set a precedent that affects hundreds of similar characters and the games that use them. The outcome will shape who owns AI-generated content for years to come.
