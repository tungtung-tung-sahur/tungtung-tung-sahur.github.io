About Tung Tung Tung Sahur

The complete story behind the brainrot icon — from a single DALL-E prompt in Indonesia to a Fortnite skin, a federal lawsuit, and a global stealth adventure.

🇮🇩 Who Is Noxa?

The creator behind the character. He is not a professional artist, and he never drew a single frame of the character by hand. He described what he wanted to an AI, named it, and published the result.

The character was created by Fernanda Bagas Indrastata, known on TikTok as @noxaasht or simply Noxa[reference:0]. He is an Indonesian content creator who, in early 2025, began posting AI-generated characters as part of a local trend called “Anomali AI character”[reference:1]. The concept was simple: generate an absurd image with DALL-E, give it an equally absurd name, and post it as a short video.

Noxa did not design the character’s visual appearance by hand. Instead, he wrote a prompt and let the AI produce the image. The character that emerged was an anthropomorphic kentongan — a traditional Indonesian slit drum used during Ramadan to wake people for sahur, the pre-dawn meal[reference:2]. Noxa gave it a humanoid body, a wide-eyed expression, and a baseball bat. He also gave it a name that mimicked the rhythmic sound of the drum: Tung Tung Tung Sahur.

📅

First Appearance

February 28, 2025 — the day before Ramadan began in Indonesia[reference:3].

✏️

Creation Method

DALL-E AI image generation. Noxa supplied the name and the prompt; the AI produced the visual[reference:4].

🥁

Cultural Inspiration

The kentongan, a slit drum used across Indonesia to wake people for sahur during Ramadan[reference:5].

🌍

Meme Category

Classified as part of the “Italian brainrot” genre despite its Indonesian origin[reference:6].

📈 How It Became a Global Phenomenon

The character did not go viral on its own. It spread through a specific ecosystem of AI-generated memes, and it exploded when a Roblox developer built an entire game around collecting them.

The initial TikTok clip was short — around 24 seconds[reference:7]. The character taps at a door and calls out for sahur in a distorted voice. The audio and the visual were strange enough to be shared widely, and the character became one of the most recognizable figures in the broader brainrot trend: a style of absurdist internet characters that swept through Generation Alpha and Generation Z in 2025[reference:8].

But the real turning point came when a Brazilian independent developer named SpyderSammy launched Steal a Brainrot on Roblox in May 2025[reference:9]. The game’s premise was simple: collect and trade brainrot characters. Tung Tung Sahur was one of the most valuable collectibles. The game exploded, hitting a peak of 24.13 million concurrent players — a record that exceeded Fortnite’s previous high of 15.3 million[reference:10].

“Tung Tung Sahur was one of the most popular characters in the game. The game itself reached over 24 million concurrent players — a record for a single game on Roblox.”

GameLook, industry report on the Steal a Brainrot phenomenon

That success is what turned the character from a viral meme into a commercial asset. Once the game started generating tens of millions of dollars, multiple parties became interested in who actually owned the character[reference:11].

Mementum Lab answers the Spyder Games complaint with counterclaims of trademark infringement and unfair competition[reference:19].

⚖️ The Copyright Dispute Explained

This is the core of the legal battle. One side argues that a character made with AI has no copyrightable author. The other argues that the human who wrote the prompt is the author. No U.S. court has ruled definitively.

The dispute centers on a single question: can an AI-generated image be protected by copyright?

Mementum Lab, the French agency representing Noxa, relies on the argument that Noxa made human creative contributions. He chose the character’s name, wrote the prompts, selected the output, and built the character’s backstory[reference:20]. The agency compares this to historical viral memes like Keyboard Cat, where the human creator successfully asserted ownership[reference:21].

Spyder Games, the developer of Steal a Brainrot, argues the opposite. Its lawyer, Aaron Moss, has stated that U.S. copyright law is consistent: “If something is generated by AI without sufficient human authorship, it’s not copyrightable — and no court has disagreed with that”[reference:22]. Because the character originated from a DALL-E image, Spyder Games contends that no valid copyright exists at all.

The stakes are high for the entire brainrot ecosystem. Nearly every character in Steal a Brainrot is based on an AI-generated image from an external creator[reference:23]. If a court rules that Mementum Lab holds enforceable rights to Tung Tung Sahur, it could set a precedent that affects hundreds of similar characters and the games that use them.

🏛️

Court

U.S. District Court, Northern District of California (San Francisco)[reference:24].

📋

Case Number

Spyder Games LLC v. Mementum Lab, 5:25-cv-10248[reference:25].

⚔️

Spyder Games Argument

AI-generated content has no human author, therefore no copyright exists[reference:26].

🛡️

Mementum Argument

Noxa’s prompt-writing, naming, and backstory creation qualify as human authorship[reference:27].

🎨 Why the Character Looks the Way It Does

The design is not random. Every element maps to a real Indonesian tradition or a deliberate creative choice.

The character’s body is shaped like a kentongan, a hollow wooden slit drum. In Indonesia, these drums are struck in neighborhoods during Ramadan to wake residents for sahur. The sound is rhythmic and repetitive — which is why the name repeats “tung” multiple times[reference:31].

The baseball bat in his hand is a modernization. Historically, the kentongan was struck with a wooden mallet or stick. Noxa chose a baseball bat, which gives the character a more aggressive, recognizable silhouette. The wide, staring eyes are a hallmark of the brainrot aesthetic — they make the character look simultaneously confused and threatening[reference:32].

The character’s full in-universe name is “Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Tung Sahur” — nine “tungs” in total. The shortened version used in the games drops the repetition. Fans sometimes refer to him as TTT Sahur or Triple T[reference:33].
