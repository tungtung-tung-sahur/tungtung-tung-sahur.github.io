Lore 5 questions

The word "tung" is onomatopoeia for the sound of a kentongan being struck. "Sahur" refers to the pre-dawn meal eaten by Muslims before fasting during Ramadan. The full name is a direct reference to the drum that wakes people for that meal — the sound itself is the character's name.

Both, depending on the interpretation. In the original lore, Tung Tung Sahur is a punishment — he comes for those who ignore the sahur call. In fan content and some games, he is portrayed as a protector or a neutral force. The ambiguity is part of why the character works across so many different genres.

The bat is a modernization of the traditional kentongan mallet. Historically, kentongans are struck with a wooden stick or mallet. Replacing the mallet with a baseball bat gives the character a more aggressive silhouette while keeping the functional purpose — it is the tool that produces the sound.

The full name — with nine repetitions of "tung" — mimics the actual rhythm of a kentongan drum during Ramadan. The nine beats are not random. They match the count used in some Indonesian villages to signal the start of sahur.

Yes, but he is not Italian. The "Italian brainrot" label refers to the fake Italian voiceovers used in most of the videos, not the actual origin of the characters. Tung Tung Sahur was created by an Indonesian creator and simply joined the same meme family as Tralalero Tralala and Bombardiro Crocodilo.

Technical 5 questions

The browser version runs on any device with a modern browser, including budget Android phones. The mobile app versions recommend Android 8.0 or iOS 14 or newer for smooth performance. The 3D Horror version is the heaviest and requires more memory — on very old devices it may stutter.

Three common causes. First: too many browser tabs open in the background. Second: hardware acceleration disabled in your browser settings. Third: the device is in power-saving mode, which limits GPU performance. Closing background tabs alone fixes the lag for most players.

The browser version requires an internet connection. The mobile app versions can be played offline once installed, except for leaderboard features and cloud save synchronization. If you plan to play on a plane or in a low-signal area, install the mobile app version in advance.

Coins, unlocked tools, and purchased upgrades save automatically after every run. Unlocked relics and story progress save the moment you find them. There is no manual save option. If you close the game mid-run, that run is lost — but everything you earned in previous runs is preserved.

No. The browser version uses keyboard and mouse only. The mobile apps use touch controls only. No version of any Tung Tung Sahur game supports gamepads or controllers. This is unlikely to change — the games are designed around the precision of mouse aiming and touch tapping.

Legal 4 questions

Legally, it is currently treated as unprotectable under U.S. copyright law because the character originated from an AI-generated image. That said, the licensing agency representing the creator has filed counterclaims arguing for trademark protection. The legal status is unresolved and being tested in federal court.

In September 2025, Mementum Lab — the licensing agency representing the original creator — filed a cease-and-desist claim. The developer of Steal a Brainrot removed the character rather than immediately fight the claim. It was later reinstated during an Admin Abuse event and has remained in the game since.

Because the legal status is unresolved, there is no clear answer. Non-commercial fan projects — artwork, videos, memes — have never been targeted. Commercial projects that generate revenue using the character have been challenged. If your project is commercial and you plan to use the exact character design, consult a lawyer before publishing.

Three parties claim or have claimed ownership. The original creator Noxa claims the human creative elements — the name, the prompt, the backstory. Mementum Lab claims to hold the licensing rights assigned by Noxa. Spyder Games argues that no one can own an AI-generated character under U.S. copyright law. The court has not yet ruled.
